Masters of the Green: The Black Caddies of Augusta National

By Latria Graham | April/May 2024

The history of Augusta National is connected to the Black caddies who helped shape the game of golf. Their work and experiences are an important part of golf history.

A History Often Overlooked

For many years, Black caddies worked at Augusta National Golf Club, carrying golf bags and helping players navigate the course. Their knowledge of the game made them valuable to the golfers they served.

Although their contributions were important, the experiences of these workers were not always recognized in the broader history of professional golf.

More Than Carrying Golf Bags

Caddies needed to understand the course, judge distances, study the terrain, and offer advice to golfers. The job required experience, patience, and a strong understanding of golf.

Their stories also reflect the racial inequalities that shaped American sports and society. Looking at their experiences helps provide a fuller understanding of Augusta National's history.

Remembering Their Contributions

Remembering these caddies means recognizing the people behind the traditions of golf. Their labor, knowledge, and experiences deserve a place in the story of the sport.

Their stories are an important part of understanding the history of golf and the people who helped build its traditions.
